Good to know

Nourishment & kitchen wisdom

  • Rich in water and fibre, lettuce is gentle on digestion and wonderfully hydrating.
  • Darker leaves (romaine, red-leaf) carry more vitamins A and K than pale crispheads.
  • Folate in the leaves supports healthy cells — a quiet gift in every salad.
  • Very low in calories, so it stretches a meal generously and kindly.
  • Crisphead and romaine hold up to warm dressings, grilling and Caesar salads.
  • Butterhead's soft leaves make elegant wraps and cups for fillings.
  • Loose-leaf types shine raw — dress them just before serving so they stay perky.
  • Tear leaves by hand rather than cutting to keep the edges from browning.
  • Wash, spin dry, then wrap loosely in a clean towel inside a container.
  • Store in the fridge's crisper drawer, away from apples and bananas (their ethylene wilts leaves).
  • Revive a limp head by soaking torn leaves in ice water for 15 minutes.
  • Most lettuce keeps its best crunch for 5–7 days when stored dry and cool.
  • Myth: "Lettuce has no nutrition." Truth: darker leaves are full of vitamins A and K.
  • Myth: "All lettuce tastes the same." Truth: flavours range from sweet butterhead to peppery leaf types.
  • Myth: "You can't grow it indoors." Truth: leaf lettuce thrives on a bright windowsill.
  • Myth: "Bolted lettuce is ruined." Truth: it turns bitter, but the seeds it makes will feed next season.
